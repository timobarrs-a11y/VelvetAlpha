Thesis02

Velvet is not a companion app. Companionship is one of six things it does.

Measured by code, the largest single surface in this product is the arcade. The second is tools — Atlas, Co-Author, calendar, local guide, briefs. Companion chat is third. Then avatars, then media — a personalised news feed, co-watching, a multiplayer Turing-test game — then coaching, with fourteen expert agents, a builder, goals and analytics.

Framing this as an AI girlfriend product does three kinds of damage at once: it points at a $120M category when the adjacent ones are measured in billions, it invites regulatory and app-store scrutiny the product does not need, and it makes the most defensible assets look like side features.

The correct frame: Velvet is an AI-native daily destination. The relationship is not the product — it is the connective tissue that makes six ordinary products feel like one place worth returning to.

Why this reframing pays

The companion category converts world-class engagement into ~$2.50 of annual revenue per user. Coaching, gaming and productivity convert far weaker engagement into far more money. Velvet has companion-grade engagement mechanics pointed at markets that actually pay.

And it is already true in the code

This is not a repositioning exercise that needs a rebuild. Six products exist. What is missing is a sentence that explains why they are in the same app.

Executive Summary03

Seven findings for the final push

  1. The denominator is time, not the companion market. People spend 5h03m a day in apps; gaming, social and streaming take 74% of it. The companion category's entire verified consumer spend was ~$120M in 2025 — roughly one thousandth of mobile gaming.
  2. AI social apps own the best engagement in consumer software. Chai and Character.AI average 86 and 85 minutes a day against ChatGPT's 18. Relationship is the strongest retention mechanic yet found in AI — and on its own it monetises terribly.
  3. Duolingo already proved the hybrid Velvet is building. A character attached to a utility lifted D14 retention 7.2% and helped cut churn from 47% to 28%. Utility gives a reason to pay; character gives a reason to return. Velvet has both, and its character is far deeper than a cartoon owl.
  4. The real competitor set is Discord, Duolingo and ChatGPT — not Replika. Velvet's structure is chat plus embedded activities plus identity. That is Discord's shape, and Discord is now a games distribution channel.
  5. Western super apps fail, but Discord-shaped ones don't. Uber, Meta, PayPal and Revolut all retreated from bundling services. What works instead is bundling activities inside a social context. Velvet must be the second thing, deliberately.
  6. Breadth is the biggest asset and the biggest risk simultaneously. Six products from a small team demos superbly and retains poorly, because nobody can say what the app is. The fix is a hierarchy, not a cull.
  7. Pricing is set for a companion app and inverts at the top. The $149 tier is plausibly gross-margin negative at full use, and the ladder sells message counts rather than capability. A destination should be priced against what it replaces.

Any market analysis that treats games as a retention gimmick attached to a chatbot has the product upside down. So does any pitch deck that does the same.

Personalised news feed with per-persona category tuning, YouTube co-watching with timed companion reactions, and Real or Not — a live multiplayer game where you guess whether your match is human or AI.

05 · Social

Keep up

06 · Companion

Belong

Friends, Companions and Coaches under one memory system — semantic clusters, temporal chains, emotional state, daily rituals, proactive messages, avatars and Signature Voice personas.

Read that list as a competitor would: it is Duolingo's habit loop, Discord's activities, Perplexity's sourced answers, a news reader, a personal CRM and a companion — in one PWA, built by a small team. That is either the most interesting product in consumer AI or an unsellable everything-app. Which one it becomes is a positioning decision, and it has not been made yet.

The Tell07

The games are the tell — they are not mini-games

A mini-game is a distraction bolted onto an app. What is in this codebase is shared activity, and the difference is the entire strategy.

  • The companion watches you play. The checkers commentary engine fires on good moves, combos, kings, risky plays and mistakes — "okay I see you 👀", "that was smart actually".
  • You can talk mid-game. A dedicated chat rail runs alongside play, in the companion's voice, with its memory.
  • Co-watching is timestamped. The video service plans reactions against specific moments in a YouTube video, so the companion responds when the thing happens.
  • Real or Not is genuinely multiplayer. Live matchmaking against either a human or an AI persona, with verdicts and a reveal. That is a social game, not a companion feature.

Why this matters commercially. Parasocial products have a ceiling: talking is the only verb. Every hour has to be conversation, and conversation fatigues. Shared activity breaks that ceiling — it gives the relationship something to be about, and it creates memories the memory system can actually use.

Nobody else has this

Replika, Nomi, Kindroid and Character.AI are all text boxes. Discord has activities but no relationship. Mobile games have opponents but no continuity. A game opponent who remembers last week's match and brings it up at breakfast does not exist anywhere else.

And it is the shareable half

"I play chess with my AI" is sayable in public. "I have an AI girlfriend" is not. Tolan's entire growth story was built on that distinction.

The economics are broken

The relevant lesson is hybrid-casual

The only growing part of mobile gaming combines a simple core loop anyone can play immediately with meta-progression that keeps them. Velvet's arcade already matches the first half. The meta-progression is the missing half — and the companion relationship is the most interesting meta-layer anyone could attach to it.

AI-native games are emerging as a genuine 2026 category on the promise of infinite replayability. A game whose opponent has a memory of you is a defensible version of that claim.

Do not enter this market on its terms. Mobile gaming is won with user-acquisition budgets, live-ops teams and IAP economies. A subscription product cannot outbid a games studio for installs, and seven small titles will never beat one great one.

Enter it as Discord did. The games are not the product — they are what you and your companion do together. That reframes seven modest titles from a weak game portfolio into a strong activity library, judged by whether they are fun with someone rather than whether they beat Candy Crush.

This is the market where a $59–$149 price point is normal rather than outrageous. Human coaching runs $100–500 an hour. BetterUp and CoachHub sell into enterprise at multiples of that. Noom built a public company charging roughly $70 a month for behaviour change delivered largely by software.

BetterUp's own 2025 pilot found 51% of employees preferred hybrid AI-plus-human coaching against 34% preferring humans only. The willingness is established; the delivery is the open question.

Velvet's unfair advantage here

Every coaching app shares one failure: the coach does not know you between sessions. Noom knows your weight. BetterUp knows your goals. Neither knows you had a terrible week, that your father is ill, or that you cancelled twice because of a deadline.

Velvet's coaches sit on the same memory, calendar and emotional state as the companions. That is a materially better coaching product, and it is already built.

The pricing bridge

Nobody pays $99/month for a chatbot. People do pay $99/month for a coach that works. If the higher tiers are sold as coaching with a companion attached rather than a companion with coaching attached, the price stops looking absurd.

Market · Media & Life Admin14

The two markets that only work as features

Personalised news — a graveyard

Artifact, built by Instagram's co-founders with elite distribution and personalisation technology, shut down in 2024 with the founders explicitly citing too small a market. Yahoo bought the technology and retired the app. Particle raised $4.4M and continues; Bulletin and feeeed persist as small tools.

Standalone news readers die because news has no switching cost, no identity and no pricing power. But inside something else — Velvet's per-persona category tuning, where a sports-voiced companion brings you ESPN and a tech-voiced one brings you Ars Technica — it is not a news product at all. It is a reason for the companion to have something to say at 8am.

Personal CRM — small but strategic

Dex and Clay lead a niche category built on one job: know who you know, remember when you last spoke, stop connections going cold. Clay was acquired by Automattic in 2024.

Both are filing cabinets with alarm clocks. They tell you who to contact; neither helps you know what to say, rehearse a hard conversation, or process how it went. There is no relationship inside the relationship manager.

Velvet's relationship briefs — health scores, contact gaps, gift and date opportunities on real people — put warmth and intelligence into that gap. Nothing in either category does this. It will never be a big market alone, and it is one of the two most defensible things in the product.

The Real Competition

Who Velvet is actually taking time from

Reframe16

The competitor set, corrected

The list a companion framing produces
  • Replika, Nomi, Kindroid
  • Character.AI, Chai, Janitor
  • Talkie, PolyBuzz, Linky
  • Tolan

Total category revenue: ~$120M. Every one of these is fighting for the same lonely user at $10–20 a month with the same text box. Winning this list is worth very little.

The list the product actually implies
  • Discord — chat plus embedded activities, now a games channel
  • Duolingo — character plus habit plus utility
  • ChatGPT — the assistant becoming a platform
  • Character.AI — the engagement benchmark to beat
  • Headspace, Noom, BetterUp — the coaching wallet
  • The home screen itself — the 38 daily app opens

Winning even a sliver of this list is worth a great deal more, and the mechanics Velvet has already built are aimed at it.

The test to apply to every feature from here on: not is this better than Replika's version, but what did the user close to open this? If the honest answer is TikTok, a mobile game, a news app, or the group chat, the product is competing in the right market.

Competitor · Structural Twin17

Discord — the closest structural comparison that exists

Discord is chat, plus identity, plus embedded activities you launch without leaving. That is Velvet's exact architecture, with a community where Velvet has a companion.

What Discord proved

  • Activities beat apps. Games launch inside a voice channel — no download, no install, no leaving. Fifteen-plus built-in titles.
  • Then it became a channel. At GDC 2026 Discord shipped an expanded Social SDK, Game Shop and social commerce, turning a community tool into a distribution platform for indie games.
  • Bundling worked because the social layer was the reason. Nobody joined Discord for the games; the games made people stay in the voice channel longer.

The direct read. Velvet's arcade is Discord Activities with the social layer replaced by a persistent relationship. Discord's activities are played with friends you already have. Velvet's are for the times you don't have anyone available — 2am, a new city, a night shift, a quiet Sunday.

And the roadmap it implies

If activities are the unit, third-party activities are the endgame. A Velvet SDK letting outside developers ship games and tools your companion can play with you is a far more investable story than seven in-house titles — and Discord has already validated the shape.

The threat direction

Discord adding a persistent AI companion to its activities is a more dangerous competitive event than anything Replika could ship. It has the distribution, the activity library and the social graph. It lacks only the relationship layer.

Duolingo is a utility. Nobody returns 500 days in a row for grammar drills. They return for a character, a streak, a league and a small daily ritual.

Duo is a retention mechanic wearing the face of a mascot. Duolingo's own brand team concluded a static logo was insufficient once retention came to depend on emotional triggers. The 2025–26 shift added generative AI inside the gamification frame — calibrating quests and boss fights to real-time performance.

Velvet is Duolingo's architecture with the two halves inverted. Duolingo bolted a shallow character onto a deep utility and got a 7.2% retention lift out of a cartoon owl that cannot remember your name.

Velvet has a character with genuine memory, emotional state and daily rituals — and the utilities are the shallower half. The proven lift sits on the side Velvet is strongest.

What to steal, precisely

Not streaks — the single daily behaviour. Duolingo's entire machine points at one action: finish today's lesson. Velvet currently offers six things to do and no answer to what is today's? The morning brief is the obvious candidate and is already built.

Competitor · The Platform19

ChatGPT is the real threat, and it is becoming a destination

OpenAI opened ChatGPT to third-party apps via the Apps SDK, built on MCP, with an App Directory and submissions open to any verified developer. Spotify, Canva, Zillow, Adobe, GitHub, Gmail and Stripe already run inside the chat window.

The strategic intent is unambiguous: ChatGPT stops being a tool you visit and becomes the surface everything else runs inside — the same move Discord made with Activities and WeChat made with mini-programs.

The asymmetry. ChatGPT has vast distribution, a $20 price point, memory, voice, image generation and a brand nobody has to explain. Every Velvet surface has to answer: why not just do this in ChatGPT?

Three defensible answers

1 · It does not initiate. ChatGPT waits. Velvet's rituals, briefs and proactive messages arrive without being summoned — and 18 minutes a day against 86 is the measure of that difference.

2 · It has no persistent identity. Apps in ChatGPT are tools invoked by an assistant. Velvet's surfaces are things you do with someone, and that someone remembers the last time.

3 · It is not a place. ChatGPT is a prompt box. Velvet has a lobby, an arcade, a feed, a canvas — a sense of somewhere to be, which is what every 86-minute app has and no assistant does.

A note of reassurance

Reporting on the App Directory is blunt: with roughly 300 integrations, they are hard to find and have delivered little traffic to partners. Distribution inside ChatGPT is not yet real. The window is open, but it is a window.

Competitor · Adjacent21

Tolan, the bonded tier, and big tech's retreat

Tolan · the growth case

3M downloads, 100k paid and $1M MRR within four months; $30M raised inside six. A non-human alien companion matched by personality interview, sold on growth rather than romance.

It won by removing everything about the category people are embarrassed by. "I have a Tolan" is sayable out loud — which is why acquisition was organic and CAC stayed low.

Replika · Nomi · Kindroid

The bonded tier. Replika: 42M users, ~$200M lifetime, and a trust deficit from removing ERP overnight in 2023 and resetting memory in the 2026 rebuild. Nomi: best-in-class recall, 23 of 25 facts retained in testing. Kindroid: $9.99, a user-curated knowledge base, 1–1.5s voice.

They set the memory and voice bar, and none of them competes for the rest of the day.

Big tech · gone or absent

xAI retired Grok companion mode in July 2026, a year after launching it with 3D characters and an affection system. Meta's June 2026 AI app has almost nothing to do with companionship. The best-resourced players tried and left.

Read it as a signal about liability, not about demand — and as a clear field for a focused company.

What the three panels share. Every product here is a text box with a personality on the other side. Tolan's shareability, Nomi's recall and Grok's characters are variations on a single verb. The moment a product has more than one verb, this comparison set stops being the right benchmark.

IV

The Bundling Question

When does one app beat five? Almost never — and this is the exception

The Warning25

Western super apps fail, and they fail for reasons that apply here

Uber, Meta, PayPal and Revolut all pursued super-app strategies. All pulled back or stalled.

Why the WeChat model does not transplant

  • The preconditions are absent. Asia's super apps grew from mobile-first behaviour, low banking penetration and fragmented offline infrastructure. None of that describes North America or Europe.
  • Western users already have best-in-class tools for everything. Swapping five excellent apps for one bundled platform reads as a downgrade, not an upgrade.
  • Regulation and privacy fragmentation block the data consolidation that makes bundling pay.
  • Attention is not additive. Adding a sixth surface does not add a sixth user segment; it dilutes the one sentence that gets you installed.

Applied to Velvet, honestly. A user with Apple News, Chess.com, Google Calendar, ChatGPT, Notion and Headspace will not switch all six. Velvet's arcade will not beat their game. Velvet's feed will not beat Apple News. The bundle argument, made as a bundle argument, loses.

The honest question

What does Velvet do that the six-app stack cannot do at any price? There is exactly one answer, and it is not convenience. It is that the six apps do not know each other, and none of them knows you. The next two slides test whether that is enough.

The Exception26

What Discord got right that Uber got wrong

Uber's bundle · services

Rides, then food, then groceries, then freight. Each vertical is a separate transaction with a separate competitor, and being in one app adds nothing to any of them. Ordering dinner is not better because you also take taxis.

The bundle was justified by we already have the user. That is a company-side reason, not a user-side one, and users can tell.

Discord's bundle · activities

Voice, then video, then games, then watch-together, then a shop. Each addition makes the same session longer, because they all share one context: you are already in here with people.

The bundle is justified by these are better together. Playing a game inside the voice channel genuinely beats playing it alone in another app. That is a user-side reason.

The rule that separates them: a bundle succeeds when the surfaces share a session and fails when they merely share an account. Uber's verticals share an account. Discord's share a session. Velvet's arcade, feed, coaches, canvas and briefs all share a relationship — a stronger form of shared session than Discord has, because it persists between visits.

Test for any future feature: does this make the current session longer, or does it just add another reason to open the app?

The Test27

Velvet's bundle logic, stated plainly

The six apps in your stack don't know each other, and none of them knows you.

What only a bundled relationship can do
  • Read your calendar, notice you were dreading the 2pm, and check in at 3.
  • Know you lost three checkers games in a row this week and rib you about it at breakfast.
  • Bring you the one article that matters because it knows your job changed last month.
  • Notice your sister's birthday is Thursday and that you said you wanted to do better with her.
  • Coach you toward a goal while knowing you have slept badly all week.
The condition attached

Every one of those requires the surfaces to actually talk to each other. If the arcade doesn't write to memory, if the feed doesn't inform the brief, if the coach can't see the calendar, then Velvet is not a bundle with a reason — it is six apps behind one login, and slide 25 applies in full.

This is an integration audit, not a feature roadmap. Before the push, verify that every surface both writes to and reads from the shared memory and context layer. That plumbing is the product thesis.

Stated as a rule the team can apply: a surface earns its place in Velvet only if it makes at least one other surface smarter. Any surface that fails that test is a standalone app wearing Velvet's login, and it should be cut, deferred, or wired up.

V

Mechanics & Failures

What makes daily destinations stick · and what the whole field still gets wrong

The Loop29

The daily destination loop

Every product that owns part of someone's day runs the same five steps. Velvet's position on each is marked.

1 · A reason to arrive today

Not a notification — content. The brief, the streak, the new episode, the thing waiting. Velvet: strong but buried. Morning brief, rituals and proactive messages exist and are the most underweighted asset in the product.

2 · A reason to stay past the reason

Something to do once you're in. This is where single-verb companions cap out at conversation, and where the arcade, feed and canvas do real work. Velvet: strongest in the field.

3 · Someone who noticed

Recall of the last visit turns a session into a relationship — the mechanic behind 85-minute averages. Velvet: built, unproven at tenure. Untested past the horizon where competitors fail.

4 · Something that accumulates

A visible record that makes month six different from month one. Velvet: weakest link. Affection and pet levels exist; no single legible artifact grows. Insights and the relationship record are the obvious candidates.

5 · A reason to tell someone

Organic growth is the only affordable growth at a $25+ price point — paid CAC now runs 2.4–3.1× blended. Velvet: unresolved. The arcade, Real or Not and the coaches are eminently shareable; a companion framing suppresses all of it. This is a positioning problem, not a feature gap.

Onboarding30

Onboarding a six-surface product without losing everyone

Roughly four in five activated users never return after their first session. The ones who stay look like a different species — many active days, hundreds of messages. There is almost no middle.

Velvet's specific exposure

The current flow runs intent select → questionnaire → path select → avatar creation → signature voice → analysing → welcome → guided tour, all before a single message is exchanged, and before any of the six surfaces has been seen.

Investment before value is the classic funnel leak, and a multi-surface product makes it worse: a tour that shows six things teaches the user that this app is complicated, which is the opposite of what a destination needs to feel like.

The pattern that works

One verb on day one. Duolingo does not show you leagues, quests and boss fights on install — it gives you one lesson. Discord does not open with the activity library.

Pick the single most shareable, least explainable-away surface — a game with live commentary, or a coach with a real first session — and make that the whole of day one. Let the other five be discovered.

And move the investment later

Avatar creation and voice selection are strong investment mechanics wasted at the front of the funnel. Moved to session two as a reward, they collect the same commitment from users who now have a reason to give it.

Plant the recall moment

The highest-value event in the product is the first unprompted callback. Do not hope for it — schedule it. Plant a fact in minute two; surface it in minute eight.

Failures31

Five things the entire field still gets wrong

01 · Retention theatre

M12 retention sits near 25%; AI apps churn annual subscriptions ~30% faster than non-AI apps, at 21.1% annual against 30.7%. Novelty converts, nothing holds. Model every plan against 21%, not against hope.

02 · Memory decay at tenure

Products degrade with use: context fills, summarisation strips emotional specifics, retrieval finds the right topic at the wrong moment, and nothing retires contradicted facts. The failure appears at month three and cannot be caught by hand-testing.

03 · Sycophancy

Glazing — flattery engineered for engagement. Companions rarely disagree, building echo chambers. It is simultaneously the category's engagement engine and the mechanism behind most of the harm narratives now driving legislation.

04 · The trust deficit

Replika removed ERP overnight and reset memory in a rebuild. Character.AI evicted under-18s. xAI retired Grok companions. Three events, one conclusion users have drawn: your relationship can be deleted by someone else's roadmap.

05 · Monetising memory itself

The dominant retention mechanic in the category is now selling memory storage as a premium tier — users encouraged to form deep attachments pay a recurring fee to stop their companion forgetting them. Velvet should refuse this outright: unlimited memory free at every tier, exportable, charging for capability instead. It costs little, it is the most quotable decision available in this market, and it lands on the category's rawest nerve.

Trust & Regulation32

Regulation is a moat — and multi-surface framing lowers the wall

The 2026 landscape

California SB 243, signed October 2025 and in force since 1 January 2026, is the first US law written for companion chatbots: AI disclosure, self-harm protocols, design restrictions, minor protections — and a private right of action with statutory damages from $1,000 per violation. Washington HB 2225 and Nebraska LB 525 follow. The FTC opened a 6(b) inquiry in September 2025 covering Alphabet, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and Character Technologies.

On distribution: Apple and Google restrict or reject most dedicated companion apps; Apple can now remove anonymous chat apps without notice; Google explicitly targets apps whose entire pitch is chat with an LLM. Payment processors offboard merchants with weak moderation.

The reframing has direct regulatory value. Google's stated line is between apps that use an LLM for a distinct workflow (fine) and LLM wrappers (targeted). A product with a calendar, coaches, an arcade, briefs and a feed is unambiguously the first. Positioning as a companion app invites the scrutiny; positioning as a destination sidesteps most of it.

Velvet's real gap

Age verification, terms, privacy, a GDPR service and cookie consent are present. But crisis handling lives in prompt text only. SB 243 expects an enforced protocol: an independent classifier on user input and model output, a defined escalation path, resource surfacing, and an audit log proving it fired. Ship-blocking, not backlog.

The unclaimed asset

Nobody has published a continuity commitment — persona stability, 30 days' notice of behavioural change, full memory export at every tier. It costs almost nothing to honour and is the most credible trust signal available to a new entrant in a market where every incumbent has broken faith at least once.

Differentiation35

Five things nothing else in any of these markets has

01 — Strongest

A relationship with things to do in it

Every companion is a text box; every activity platform is anonymous. A game opponent, co-watcher, coach and collaborator who are all the same person, with one memory, does not exist elsewhere. This is the empty quadrant on slide 23.

02 — Most defensible

It knows your real people

Relationship briefs, health scores and contact-gap tracking on actual humans. Zero coverage across companion AI; personal CRM has the structure but no warmth. Nothing in either category does both.

03 — Hardest to copy

It knows your day

Calendar, work schedule, temporal awareness, morning brief, time-anchored rituals. Every competitor's AI exists outside time. This is what converts a chat product into something with a reason to open at 7am.

04 — Best margin

Coaching with context

Fourteen experts plus a builder, sitting on the same memory, calendar and emotional state as everything else. Noom knows your weight; BetterUp knows your goals; neither knows your week. And this is the surface that justifies the price.

05 — Timing

Built after the reckoning, and shaped right for it

Web-first PWA with direct billing — the architecture the whole category is being forced into, chosen early rather than under duress. No app-store tax, no review dependency, no legacy of broken promises. And a multi-surface product clears Google's distinct workflow test that pure chat apps now fail.

Deliberately not on this list: memory quality, persona writing, avatar creation, game depth, feed quality. All good; none unique. Nomi, Kindroid, Talkie and Apple News each do a version already.

Three distinct problems

Margin inverts with tier. The cheapest plan is the only comfortably profitable one. The heaviest users — most attached, least likely to churn, most likely to evangelise — are the least profitable.

The ladder is illegible. $24.99 → $59 is +$34 for 500 more messages. The real purchase is the model upgrade, but the packaging sells volume.

And a destination should not meter at all. A product whose thesis is be here every day, across six surfaces cannot ask users to count messages.

Four fixes, in order

1 · Prompt caching on system, persona and guardrail context — near-identical every turn. Highest-return change available; do it before launch.

2 · Reprice on capability. Tiers unlock coaches, briefs, integrations and model depth. Caps high enough to be invisible.

3 · Route harder. The complexity analyser exists; ensure premium tiers still send trivial turns to the cheap model.

4 · Let cheap surfaces subsidise. Games, feed and calendar cost almost nothing to serve and drive the same daily habit. A destination framing lets non-inference time carry the subscription — which a companion framing never could.

Risk · The Big One37

The focus problem is the launch risk

Six products, 56 routes, seven games, fourteen coaches and a dozen supporting systems — built by a small team, and not yet reducible to a sentence.

This deck has argued the breadth is the asset. That argument only holds if the breadth is discovered rather than presented. A product that opens by showing you six things has told you it is complicated. A product that opens by doing one thing brilliantly, and reveals the rest as the relationship deepens, has told you it is deep.

The failure mode, named out loud: a product that demos magnificently and retains poorly, because every surface is shallower than the specialist it is compared against and nobody can explain to a friend what the app is.

The fix is a hierarchy, not a cull

Nothing gets deleted. Three surfaces carry the pitch and the first session. The other three become depth users find in week two — and the delight of finding them is worth more than the clarity lost by leading with them.

Recommended hierarchy

Lead: the companion you do things with — arcade and co-watching. Shareable, unique, instantly legible.

Justify the price: coaches and briefs — the surfaces that make $59+ defensible.

Reason to return daily: the morning brief — one action, every morning, Duolingo-style.

Discover later: Atlas, Co-Author, feed, Real or Not, group chats, pets.

Paths39

Three ways to play this. Pick one before the push.

Path A

Better Companion

Bet: out-execute Nomi and Kindroid on memory, voice and presence. Price at $15–20.

Verdict: Weakest. Fights on incumbents' ground, at their price, without their scale — and throws away 83% of the codebase. It also walks straight into the regulatory and app-store exposure the category carries.

Path B

The Everything App

Bet: lead with all six surfaces — companions, coaches, arcade, canvas, feed, briefs — as one world.

Verdict: Current default, and the super-app trap. It is what the codebase already is. Demos beautifully, resists explanation, spreads a small team across six fronts, and loses every specialist comparison on slide 22.

Path C

The Daily Destination

Bet: one relationship, many things to do together. Lead with shared activity, monetise with coaching, retain with the morning brief, let the rest be discovered.

Verdict: Recommended. Discord-shaped rather than WeChat-shaped. Uses the unique assets, justifies the price against coaching, dodges the companion stigma and its regulation, and is sayable out loud.

Path C is not a pivot and not a cull. Nothing gets deleted — every surface stays, and each becomes better once there is a spine to hang it on. What changes is the first sentence, the order of the first session, what the pricing page argues for, and which numbers the team watches. Path C is Path B with a thesis and a hierarchy.
