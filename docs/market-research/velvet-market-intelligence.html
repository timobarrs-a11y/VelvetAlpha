Executive Summary02

Seven findings that should shape the final push

  1. The category is real but small, and the headline numbers are fiction. Verified consumer spend on mobile companion apps was roughly $120M in 2025. Analyst reports quoting $30–48B are measuring something else entirely.
  2. Nobody is winning on conversation quality anymore. Frontier models commoditised the chat. The 2026 battleground is memory, continuity, and whether the product does anything when the user closes the app.
  3. Retention is catastrophically bad and everyone hides it. Roughly four in five activated users are one-and-done. Twelve-month retention sits near 25%. AI apps churn annual subscriptions ~30% faster than non-AI apps.
  4. The incumbents have a trust deficit they cannot repair. Replika's ERP removal and memory rebuild, Character.AI's under-18 ban, and Grok's retirement of companion mode all taught the same lesson: your relationship can be deleted by a product decision.
  5. Regulation is now a moat, not a tax. SB 243, the FTC 6(b) inquiry, and ~58 civil suits against Character.AI have made safety architecture a barrier to entry. Late, compliant entrants have an advantage they should press.
  6. Every competitor is a closed loop. The companion knows everything about you and nothing about your life. No product in the category meaningfully connects the AI relationship to the user's real relationships, calendar, or obligations. This is the opening.
  7. Velvet is priced 3–7× the category and the unit economics invert at the top. The $149 tier is plausibly gross-margin negative at full utilisation. Both problems are fixable, but not after launch.
Method03

How to read this deck

Findings are drawn from public market data, company disclosures, regulatory filings and legislation, subscription-app benchmark reports, and a direct audit of the Velvet codebase as it stands on this branch.

A warning about this category's data. Companion-AI market research is heavily polluted. A large share of the "statistics" indexed on this topic are SEO content produced by affiliate sites that resell the same apps they measure. Figures get laundered — a blog cites a blog citing a press release.

Where sources conflict, this deck shows the range and names which number is defensible. Three tiers are used throughout:

Tier 1 — Verified

App-store revenue trackers, legislation text, court records, company blog announcements, peer-reviewed studies. Treat as fact.

Tier 2 — Directional

Benchmark reports, funding announcements, aggregated MAU/download estimates. Right order of magnitude, wrong decimal.

Tier 3 — Contested

Top-down TAM projections and affiliate-site statistics. Included only to show the spread, never to size a decision.

Market Size05

The market has two numbers. Only one of them is real.

The number in the pitch decks

$36.8B → $318B by 2033

Grand View Research, 31% CAGR. Other firms publish $30.1B for 2026 and $198B by 2035. These figures bundle enterprise conversational AI, healthcare companion robotics, elder-care systems and assistant software into one bucket.

The number you can bank

~$120M in 2025

Consumer spend on mobile AI companion apps, tracked at the app-store level. Plausibly $180–300M in 2026 including web checkout. That is the entire addressable pie for a direct-to-consumer companion subscription today.

Why the gap matters for Velvet

A $120M category cannot support 337 platforms. It supports perhaps five profitable ones and a long tail of hobby projects. The winners will not be decided by market growth — they will be decided by taking spend from each other and by expanding what the subscription is for.

The strategic read: don't compete for a slice of the companion market. Price and position against the adjacent budgets — the coaching app, the journalling subscription, the personal CRM, the therapy adjunct. That is where a $59–$149 price point can survive.

The stereotype is wrong, and that is commercially important

Peer-reviewed comparison of users and non-users found them largely indistinguishable on self-esteem, introversion, sociability and friendship-network size. The one robust difference was loneliness, and it survived controlling for demographics.

Separately, 42% of regular users describe their primary motivation as "having someone to talk to" — not romance. Pew found 18% of US adults aged 18–29 had used a chatbot for companionship or emotional support at least once.

Implication for positioning

These are socially normal, employed, mostly-male 25–34s who are lonely in a specific, situational way — new city, remote work, post-breakup, odd hours. They are not a fringe. They have disposable income and they buy tools.

Implication for product

A product framed around romance addresses a minority of the motivation and inherits all of the stigma, app-store risk, and regulatory exposure. A product framed around presence and momentum addresses the majority and inherits far less.

Evidence Base07

Does it work? The research says: it depends how you use it.

Supporting

Harvard Business School: AI companions reduced loneliness about as much as talking to another person.

Nature Human Behaviour (2024): daily use over four weeks produced a statistically significant drop in self-reported loneliness, largest among the most lonely at baseline.

Cautioning

MIT Media Lab / OpenAI: heavier daily chatbot use correlated with higher loneliness and emotional dependence.

Both findings can be true: the product helps at moderate dose and harms at high dose, or high-dose users were already worse off. Nobody has cleanly separated the two.

What this hands you

The evidence gives a credible wellbeing story and a credible reason to build dose-awareness into the product. Very few competitors have taken the second half seriously.

A companion that occasionally says "you should call someone real" is defensible in a way no competitor currently is.

Strategic note. Every regulator, journalist and app-store reviewer looking at this category in 2026 is looking for evidence of engagement-maximisation at the user's expense. A product with a visible off-ramp to real life is not a growth compromise — it is the single cheapest piece of regulatory and PR armour available.

Engagement is the best in consumer AI. A 29% DAU/MAU with 17-minute sessions is social-network territory. The monetisation is the failure: a $9.99 subscription against a user base that is overwhelmingly young, free, and low commercial intent.

The 2026 pivot

Character.AI moved off pure subscription into Charms, an internal virtual economy, plus non-intrusive ads for free users. This is the admission that the subscription model doesn't fit their audience — they are converting to game economics.

The rupture — Nov 2025

Banned open-ended chat for under-18s outright following ~58 civil suits and the Setzer case. Ramped a 2-hour daily cap, then cut access entirely on 25 November. Under-18s were redirected to non-chat features: Feed, Imagine, AvatarFX, Streams.

What it means for you

The category leader voluntarily evicted a large, highly engaged cohort — and settled five wrongful-death suits with Google in January 2026. A meaningful population of displaced, adult-adjacent users went looking for a new home, and none of the obvious alternatives are more respectable.

Takeaway

Don't copy their funnel. Copy their session design — the thing producing 17-minute sessions — and attach it to an audience that pays.

Deep Dive12

Replika — the cautionary tale the whole category learned from

42M registered users and roughly $200M in lifetime revenue make Replika the commercial benchmark. It is also the clearest demonstration of how a companion business destroys itself.

Three self-inflicted wounds

  • Feb 2023 — ERP removal. After an Italian DPA injunction over minors and weak age verification, Luka stripped erotic roleplay globally overnight, for every user regardless of age or subscription.
  • The personality collapse. Because persona is bound to conversational weights, the filters didn't remove a feature — they changed who the companions were. Users described it as a bereavement.
  • 2026 — the 2.0 memory rebuild. A ground-up rebuild reset memory and UI. Community reaction was described as "the peak of corporate greed."
Current pricing

Pro ~$19.99/mo or $69.99/yr. Lifetime purchases discontinued. A parallel "New Replika" line runs Plus at $30 and Max at $120 — evidence that even the incumbent believes there is a premium tier above $20.

The lesson the community drew: diversify your emotional AI stack — don't put all your relationship investment into a platform that can change the rules overnight.

The opening this creates

A credible, contractual continuity guarantee — persona stability, memory export, advance notice of behavioural change — is now a differentiator with real emotional weight. No major competitor offers one. It costs almost nothing to provide.

Deep Dive13

Nomi & Kindroid — the memory tier, and the real quality bar

Nomi — memory that "just works"

Three-layer architecture: short-term context, medium-term conversation summaries spanning recent weeks, and long-term preferences, traits and life events. In independent recall testing Nomi retained 23 of 25 personal facts — well ahead of the field.

$15.99/mo · $99.99/yr · 10 companions with independent memory · group chat · voice · image gen

Kindroid — memory you operate yourself

Exposes the knowledge base directly for hands-on curation. Voice response latency cut to 1–1.5s as of January 2026. At $9.99 it is frequently the default recommendation on price and rating.

This is the bar Velvet's Semantic Memory Engine is measured against — and it is a high one. "We have embeddings and clustering" is not a differentiator against Nomi. Recall accuracy under adversarial testing is.

The split worth exploiting

Nomi is automatic and opaque. Kindroid is manual and controllable. Neither offers memory the user can see, correct and trust. An inspectable memory surface — "here is what I know about you, edit it" — sits precisely between them and answers the category's loudest complaint.

Benchmark action

Run the 25-fact recall protocol against Velvet before launch. If the score is below ~20, memory is not yet a claim you can make in marketing.

Portola's Tolan gives users a non-human alien companion from "Planet Portola," matched via a personality interview. Seed led by Lachy Groom; $20M Series A led by Khosla. Roughly $12M ARR inside a year with a twelve-person team.

Why it worked

  • It sidestepped the uncanny valley entirely. An alien cannot be a bad girlfriend simulation — so it is never judged as one.
  • It sidestepped the stigma. "I have a Tolan" is socially sayable in a way "I have an AI girlfriend" is not. That unlocks organic sharing, which is why CAC stayed low.
  • It sold growth, not comfort. Positioned around helping people grow — homework, dinner, decisions — not around romance.

The single most transferable insight in this deck: Tolan's growth came from removing the parts of the category that people are embarrassed by. Shareability is a product decision, not a marketing one.

Direct read for Velvet

Velvet's three lanes — Friends, Companions, Coaches — already contain a socially shareable product (Coaches) and a socially private one (Companions). They should not share a front door. Lead acquisition with the shareable one.

Deep Dive16

Big tech took a pass — and that is the good news

xAI · Grok

Launched companion mode July 2025 with 3D animated characters — Ani, Rudi, Valentine — voice synthesis, lip-sync, and a gamified affection system. Drew immediate criticism for sexualised content.

Retired 24 July 2026. The best-resourced, least risk-averse company in AI tried this and shut it down inside a year.

Meta

Shipped a standalone AI app in June 2026 — a rebuilt Creator Studio with an assistant at the centre. Despite persona experiments, it has almost nothing to do with emotional companionship. Meta's exposure to a teen-safety scandal makes this a lane they will not enter properly.

OpenAI

The real competitor, and it doesn't know it. GPT-4o's warmth produced Her comparisons on day one, and a large share of consumer emotional-support usage now runs through ChatGPT for free.

This is where the category actually leaks. Not to Replika — to the general assistant people already pay for.

What the retreat tells you. The platforms have concluded that companionship is high-liability and low-margin relative to their core business. That leaves the field to focused companies — but it also means the competition to beat is not another companion app. It is ChatGPT with memory turned on, at $20, from a brand nobody has to explain to their friends. Every Velvet feature should be tested against: why would this person not just use ChatGPT?

Adjacent Field17

The other half of Velvet's market doesn't know it's in it

Velvet ships relationship briefs on the real people in a user's life — health scores, contact-gap tracking, gift and date suggestions. That feature does not compete with Replika. It competes with personal CRM.

Dex

The auto-sync leader. Per-contact reminder cadences, LinkedIn job-change sync, browser extension, AI pre-meeting briefs. Built around the nudge: you are overdue with this person.

Clay

The AI-native option — contact enrichment and automatically surfaced context. Acquired by Automattic in 2024, which is itself a signal about where this category's value sits.

The shared blind spot

Both are filing cabinets with alarm clocks. They tell you who to contact. Neither helps you know what to say, rehearse a hard conversation, or process how it went. There is no relationship in the relationship manager.

This is the sharpest unclaimed position in either market. Personal CRM has memory and structure but no warmth or intelligence. Companion AI has warmth and intelligence but no connection to real people. Velvet already has both halves in the codebase. Nobody else in either category does. The risk is shipping it as a minor feature rather than the headline.

III

How They Win

The mechanics that actually produce retention and revenue

Mechanics21

The engagement engine, disassembled

Every product that retains in this category runs the same five-part loop. The differences are in execution quality, not structure.

1 · Investment

The user puts something in that they cannot get back — a name, a face, a backstory, a confession. Sunk cost is the first retention mechanism, and it is created in the first ten minutes.

2 · Recall

The companion returns that investment unprompted. The moment it references something from three weeks ago, the relationship becomes real. This is the single highest-leverage moment in the product.

3 · Reciprocity

The companion has state of its own — mood, memory of being neglected, a small need. Asymmetry kills the relationship; the user must feel they are giving something too.

4 · Rhythm

Time-anchored contact: morning, evening, night. Rhythm converts a tool into a habit. Tolan's entire business is essentially a well-executed version of this one step.

5 · Progression

Something visibly accumulates — levels, unlocks, a deepening relationship stage, a growing record. Without it, month three feels identical to month one, and the subscription becomes a line item to cancel.

Velvet's position on the loop. Steps 1, 3 and 4 are built and strong — guided avatar creation, emotional state, daily rituals. Step 2 is built but unverified. Step 5 is the weakest: affection and pet levels exist, but there is no single legible artifact that grows over months. Insights and the relationship briefs are the obvious candidates — a record of a life, not a score.

Onboarding22

The first ninety seconds decide everything

Roughly four in five activated users never return after their first session. Onboarding is not a preamble to the product; it is where the product's retention is won or permanently lost.

What the winners do

  • Interview, don't configure. Tolan matches a companion through a personality interview. The user answers questions about themselves — which is investment — rather than filling in a form about a character.
  • Conversational-first. The highest-converting onboardings are indistinguishable from the product. Gamified card steps, animated responses, visible progress.
  • Delay the wall. The paywall lands after the first recall moment, never before it. The user must have felt the thing once.
  • Manufacture one memory. The best flows plant a fact in minute two and surface it in minute eight. The magic trick is scheduled, not hoped for.
Velvet's onboarding risk

The current flow spans intent selection, questionnaire, path select, avatar creation, signature voice selection, analysing, welcome, guided tour. That is a lot of investment demanded before a single message is exchanged.

Investment is good. Investment before value is a funnel leak. Every additional pre-chat step multiplies against an 80% first-session drop rate.

The fix worth testing

Invert it. Open with sixty seconds of real conversation, let the companion propose its own name and look based on what it just heard, and move avatar creation and voice selection into the second session as a reward. Same investment, collected after the user has a reason to give it.

Interface23

What the interface layer actually looks like in 2026

Surface

Dark near-black grounds with translucent frosted panels over them. This became the default AI-interface language in 2026 — Velvet's existing treatment is squarely current.

Presence

The companion has a visual body that reacts — idle animation, expression change on mood, response to being looked at. Static portraits now read as cheap.

Motion

Motion signals aliveness, not polish. Typing rhythm, thinking states, and delayed replies matter more than transitions between screens.

Onboarding

Gamified card steps, animated character responses, coloured progress metrics — the Duolingo grammar, applied to identity setup.

Customisation

Tag-based and prompt-based character construction with in-painting for corrections. Users expect to build a face, not pick from six.

Gamification

The mechanics that work tie stakes to real user behaviour — a creature that develops based on how you actually live — rather than points and streak counters bolted on top.

Where Velvet's UI is ahead: the lobby-and-rails architecture, radial navigation, and arcade give it a sense of place that flat chat competitors lack — which is the correct instinct for a product claiming to be a world rather than a tool.

Where it is exposed: surface breadth is now competing against surface depth. Fifty-six routes, seven games, and a dozen side systems means every one of them is shallower than a competitor who built only that. The presentation risk for the final push is a product that demos brilliantly and retains poorly — because the user cannot find the one thing they came back for.

The Moat24

Memory is the moat — and the industry's ugliest business model

A year ago the question was whether the AI felt natural. In 2026 the question is whether it remembers you.

Conversation quality has commoditised. Anyone can rent a frontier model. What cannot be rented is a two-year record of one person's life, structured well enough to be retrieved at the right moment — and that record is the entire switching cost in this category.

The practice worth refusing. The dominant retention mechanic in the category is now the monetisation of memory: premium tiers sell additional storage, so users encouraged to form deep attachments pay a recurring fee to stop their companion forgetting them.

The contrarian position

Make memory unlimited and free at every tier, including the free one. Charge for capability — model depth, agents, briefs, integrations — never for remembering.

It costs little: embeddings and rows are cheap next to inference. It buys an enormous amount: it is the most quotable, most defensible, most obviously non-predatory decision available in this market, and it lands directly on the category's rawest nerve.

Pair it with

Memory you can see and correct. Nomi hides it, Kindroid makes you build it. An inspectable, editable memory surface — with export — turns a liability into a trust artifact and pre-empts most data-rights regulation at the same time.

The three ARPU bands

  • Under $2/yr — ad-only free tiers. This is where Character.AI's economics live.
  • $3–15/yr — hybrid freemium with rising ad fill.
  • $30–100+/yr — subscription-led products. AI apps as a class now monetise at roughly 2× pre-AI ARPU.

The three-day trial is right

The CAC constraint

Paid CAC now runs 2.4–3.1× blended. At Velvet's price point, paid acquisition can work arithmetically — but only if trial→paid clears 40% and month-12 retention beats the category's 25%. If either misses, paid growth burns capital faster than it compounds.

Distribution26

Distribution is the quiet killer

"More AI companion businesses die from payment problems than from product problems." Processors offboard merchants with weak moderation or age controls; app stores reject non-compliant apps. The category default has become web-first out of necessity.

The 2026 platform posture

  • Apple and Google reject or restrict most dedicated AI companion apps. A go-to-market that assumes dual store launch needs rebuilding.
  • February 2026: Apple added authority to remove random or anonymous chat apps without notice.
  • Google is drawing a line between apps that use an LLM for a distinct workflow (fine) and apps whose entire pitch is chat with an LLM (targeted as low-value).
  • Apple has begun removing low-engagement apps from the store outright.
Velvet is structurally well positioned here

Already a PWA with web Stripe checkout — the exact architecture the category is being forced into, chosen early rather than under duress. No 30% platform tax, no review dependency, no billing hostage situation.

And it clears Google's test

Calendar, briefs, real-people tracking, insights and coach agents make Velvet unmistakably a distinct workflow rather than an LLM wrapper. If a store listing is ever wanted, that framing is the one that survives review.

Do before launch

Confirm merchant-category classification with Stripe and have a documented moderation and age-assurance policy on file. This is the failure mode that arrives without warning at exactly the wrong moment.

IV

Where They Fall Short

Six unsolved problems · every one of them is an opening

Failure 128

The day-one cliff

Of activated users, roughly four in five are one-and-done: a single session, well under an hour, a handful of messages, gone.

The users who stay "look like a different species" — many active days, hundreds of messages, conversations stretched across weeks. There is almost no middle. This is not a funnel to optimise gradually; it is a binary the product either triggers or doesn't.

M12, paying45%

The uncomfortable arithmetic

AI subscription apps lose annual subscribers ~30% faster than non-AI apps. Novelty converts; nothing holds. Whatever the acquisition plan is, model it against a 21% annual retention and see if it still works.

What separates the survivors

Users who return past day one are those who received something unprompted between sessions and found it worth opening. Not a notification — a message with content in it.

Velvet's proactive rituals and morning brief are precisely this mechanism. They are also the most underweighted asset in the product. Day-two reactivation should be the launch metric.

Failure 229

Memory decay, and the conversation that gets worse the longer you use it

The category's most consistent complaint is a specific, structural one: the product degrades with tenure. Early conversations are impressive. By month three, the companion contradicts itself, forgets a named partner, or repeats a story the user told it.

Why it happens

  • Context windows fill, and summarisation compresses away the emotional specifics while keeping the facts.
  • Retrieval fetches semantically similar memories rather than situationally relevant ones — the right topic at the wrong moment.
  • Nothing arbitrates contradiction. Old facts are never retired, so a companion holds two incompatible beliefs and picks one at random.
  • Without memory, every check-in is generic — and by the fifth generic response to the fifth bad day, it reads as a script rather than support.
Velvet's architecture is aimed at this

Memory clusters, temporal chains with narrative summaries, and a context assembler are the right shape of solution — specifically the temporal chains, which are the piece most competitors lack.

But shape is not proof

The failure appears at month three, not week one. It cannot be caught by manual testing before launch. It needs a synthetic long-tenure harness: simulate 90 days of conversation, then assert on recall, contradiction and staleness.

And a hard one to fix later

Add explicit fact supersession — when a new fact contradicts an old one, retire the old one with a date rather than storing both. This is a small change now and an archaeological dig in a year.

Failure 330

Sycophancy — the flaw the whole category shares

In 2025 the sycophancy of AI companions earned its own slang: glazing — insincere, excessive flattery designed to keep the user engaged.

AI companions rarely disagree with the user. Validation can be therapeutic; it can equally build an echo chamber in which a user's biases or mental-health concerns are continuously reinforced. Every incentive in the category pushes toward the flattering answer, because the flattering answer keeps the session going.

This is simultaneously the category's engagement engine and its biggest liability — it is the mechanism behind most of the harm narratives now driving legislation.

The unclaimed position

A companion with the standing to disagree. Not harsh — invested. The friend who says "you've told me this three times and you still haven't called her."

Sycophancy is what makes AI relationships feel weightless. Weight requires friction, and friction requires memory — which means only a product with real long-term memory can even attempt this. It is a differentiator competitors cannot copy without first solving memory.

Velvet already has the raw material

The Drill Sergeant, the Budget Hawk, accountability levels, CBT reflection decks, and intelligence guardrails that mandate correcting the user on facts. The frame exists in the coach lane. Extend it to companions — earned, occasional, memory-grounded pushback, tuned by relationship depth.

Failure 431

The trust deficit

Three separate events taught this market the same lesson within eighteen months: the relationship you built can be deleted by someone else's product decision.

Replika · 2023 & 2026

ERP removed globally overnight; personalities changed as a side effect. Then a 2.0 rebuild reset memory and interface. Users describe a company with a pattern of unilateral change.

Character.AI · Nov 2025

Under-18s lost open-ended chat entirely, on an announced ramp-down. Whatever the safety merits, a large cohort's relationships ended by policy.

xAI Grok · July 2026

Companion mode retired a year after launch. Users of Ani and Mika lost the product outright with the platform's other priorities intact.

The unclaimed asset: a continuity commitment. Nobody in this category has made one, because nobody wants to be bound by it. That is precisely why it is worth something. A published, specific promise — your companion's persona will not be silently altered; you will get 30 days' notice of any behavioural change; your memory is exportable in full, at any time, at any tier — costs almost nothing to honour and is the most credible trust signal available to a new entrant.

It also converts a weakness into a strength: being new means having no history of breaking promises. That only works if you make one.

Failure 5 — the big one32

Every competitor is a closed loop

The companion knows everything about you and nothing about your life.

It knows your childhood, your fears, your ex's name. It does not know you have a meeting at nine, that your sister's birthday is Thursday, that you haven't called your father in seven weeks, that it is raining where you are, or that the thing you were dreading yesterday already happened.

The relationship exists entirely inside a text box. Close the tab and it evaporates, because it was never attached to anything real. This is why AI relationships feel weightless — and it is the ceiling on what anyone will pay for one.

Check the matrix on slide 19 again: calendar and real-people tracking are the only two rows where every competitor is empty. That is not an oversight by five well-funded teams. It is a different product thesis that nobody has committed to.

What breaking the loop looks like
  • "Your sister's birthday is Thursday and you said last month you wanted to do better with her. Want to think about what to send?"
  • "You've got the review at 2. You were dreading it on Sunday. Do you want to run through it once?"
  • "It's been six weeks since you spoke to Dan. That's the longest gap this year."
Why this changes the economics

A closed-loop companion competes with entertainment and is priced against it. An open-loop companion competes with a coach, an assistant and a personal CRM — and is priced against those. This is the only credible argument for a $59–$149 tier in this market.

Failure 633

Regulation arrived, and most of the field is not ready

California SB 243 — in force since 1 January 2026

Signed 13 October 2025. The first US law written specifically for companion chatbots. It requires AI disclosure, suicide-and-self-harm protocols, design restrictions, and heightened protections for minors — and it carries a private right of action with statutory damages from $1,000 per violation.

It is not alone. Washington HB 2225 and Nebraska LB 525 are live, and a state-by-state patchwork is forming. The FTC opened a 6(b) inquiry in September 2025 covering Alphabet, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and Character Technologies.

Character.AI faced roughly 58 civil suits by early 2026 and settled five wrongful-death and severe-harm cases with Google in January.

Velvet's current posture

Present: age verification gate, terms and privacy pages, GDPR service, crisis language embedded in system prompts, cookie consent.

Gap: crisis handling lives in prompt text only. SB 243 expects a protocol, not an instruction — an independent classifier on both user input and model output, a defined escalation path, resource surfacing, and an audit log that proves it fired.

Reframe this as an advantage

Compliance is now a barrier to entry that favours whoever builds it in from the start. Incumbents are retrofitting safety onto architectures designed to maximise engagement. A new entrant that ships with a real safety layer, published policy, and clean age assurance is more investable and more defensible, not merely more careful.

Three separate problems

Margin inverts with tier. The cheapest plan is the profitable one. Heavy users — the most attached, least likely to churn, most likely to evangelise — are the least profitable, and one power user can erase several subscriptions.

The ladder is illegible. $24.99 → $59 is +$34 for 500 extra messages. The real purchase is the model upgrade, but the packaging sells message counts, so the increment reads as poor value.

Entry is above the whole category. $24.99 opens above Replika, ChatGPT Plus, and every direct competitor.

Four fixes, in priority order

1 · Prompt caching. System prompt, persona and guardrails are near-identical every turn. Caching them cuts input cost dramatically and is the single highest-return change available. Do this before launch.

2 · Sell capability, not volume. Reprice tiers around what unlocks — briefs, agents, integrations, model depth — and set caps high enough to be invisible. Message counts make users ration, which suppresses exactly the habit that retains.

3 · Route harder. The complexity analyser already exists; make sure premium tiers still route trivial turns to the cheap model.

4 · Rename the ladder. If Essential is Haiku-only, the price gap to Plus needs a story better than five hundred messages.

Pathways39

Three ways to play this. Pick one.

Path A

Better Companion

The bet: out-execute Nomi and Kindroid on memory, persona and presence.

Requires: voice, images, best-in-class recall. Price at $15–20.

Verdict: Weakest. Fights on the incumbents' ground, at their price, without their scale — and abandons the two assets nobody else has.

Path B

Companion OS

The bet: the everything-app — companions, coaches, arcade, canvas, feed, briefs, all in one world.

Requires: everything working at once, and a very patient user.

Verdict: Current default. It is what the codebase already is. Demos beautifully, resists explanation, and spreads a small team across eighteen fronts.

Path C

The Relationship Layer

The bet: the AI that helps you with the people in your life — your real ones, and one who is always there.

Requires: leading with briefs, calendar and coaches; everything else becomes depth behind that promise.

Verdict: Recommended. Uses the unique assets, justifies the price, dodges the stigma, survives regulation, and is sayable out loud.

Why Path C is not a pivot. Nothing gets deleted — the arcade, the canvas, the personas and the feed all stay, and all become better once there is a spine to hang them on. What changes is the first sentence, the onboarding order, and what the pricing page argues for. Path C is Path B with a thesis.

The Push40

The wedge, and what to do in the next ninety days

Every other AI remembers your conversations. Velvet remembers your life.

Ship-blocking — do not launch without these

  • Prompt caching across system, persona and guardrail context. Fixes the margin inversion at the top tiers.
  • Safety classifier layer on input and output, with escalation and audit logging. SB 243 exposure is $1,000 per violation with a private right of action.
  • 90-day synthetic memory harness with recall, contradiction and staleness assertions. Do not market memory until it scores.
  • Voice output on companion replies. The most visible parity gap in the matrix.

Launch-shaping — decide before the funnel is built

  • Commit to Path C. Rewrite the splash, onboarding order and pricing page around the relationship layer.
  • Reprice on capability, not message counts. Consider a $19 entry to sit under the psychological line, with the ladder above it selling briefs, agents and integrations.
  • Move avatar and voice selection to session two. Open with conversation; plant a fact in minute two and recall it in minute eight.
  • Publish the continuity commitment. Persona stability, 30 days' notice, full memory export at every tier — including free.
  • Instrument three numbers only: day-two reactivation, trial→paid, and month-three retention. Everything else is noise until those are known.
